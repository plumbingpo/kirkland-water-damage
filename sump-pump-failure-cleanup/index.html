Sump Pump Failure Cleanup Help in Kirkland

Sump pumps do a single task, yet they commonly quit right when that task matters. During heavy rain the water table climbs and fills the pit, at which point an outage, a stuck float or an overwhelmed pump stops the pumping. Water rises over the rim of the pit and spreads across the basement floor. Nothing stops it until some pump is working again.

This water is not clean. It has come through soil and often carries silt, bacteria and whatever was in the drain tile, so we treat it as Category 2 at best and Category 3 if it has sat or mixed with sewage. For the job, this means soaked porous items such as drywall and carpet pad are usually discarded, and what stays gets washed and disinfected first, then dried.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Stay Out of the WaterDo not step into a flooded basement while the power is on. Water may be touching outlets, the furnace or the pump's own cord.
  2. Shut Off Basement PowerIf the breaker panel can be reached while you stand somewhere dry, switch off the circuits for the basement. Call the utility when the panel itself sits in the flooded area.
  3. Check the Pump SafelyLook to see whether the pump came unplugged, its outlet tripped or the float is pinned against the pit wall. Reaching into the pit or the water is not safe.
  4. Treat the Water as ContaminatedIf going down cannot be avoided, put on gloves and rubber boots, leave pets and children upstairs, and wash up after handling wet items.
  5. Move Valuables and CallCarry dry valuables upstairs, photograph the water level and the pump, then call us. Waiting for it to recede without help is a mistake.
The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • A pump too small for the volume of water entering the pit.
  • An intake screen packed with mud and stones so the pump cannot draw water.
  • The float getting caught on the side of the basin or tangled in its cord.
  • An old motor that burned out after years of cycling on and off.
  • No backup battery to keep the pump going once storm winds knock out power.
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

Drywall and Wall Insulation

Dirty water climbs from the slab into insulation and drywall, far higher than the line you can see. The lower part that got wet is cut away, not dried, on account of the contamination.

Low Electrical Outlets

Wall outlets near the floor are sometimes submerged in a sump flood. Before those circuits are used again, an electrician needs to inspect whatever was under water.

Basement Mechanical Equipment

Mechanical equipment often shares the floor with the sump pit. Water reaching burners, controls or wiring can ruin them, and each appliance must be inspected before it is used again.

Wall Framing

Bottom plates rest directly on concrete, so they are the first framing to get wet. We clean, disinfect and dry the studs and plates after removing the wet drywall.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Pump Out the Basement

We bring our own pumps and extractors, so nothing depends on your failed sump. Standing water and silt are removed from the floor, the pit area and any low spots.

2

Take Out What Got Wet

Wearing protective gear, we take out the carpet, pad, wet drywall and insulation that the groundwater touched, bag it and haul it away so clean framing and concrete are exposed.

3

Clean and Sanitize

The slab, wall framing and remaining contents are washed down and treated with an antimicrobial product. Doing it ahead of drying keeps the air movers from spreading contaminants through the basement.

4

Dry and Verify

Typically for 3 to 5 days, air movers and dehumidifiers stay in place, until the concrete and framing read normal on a moisture meter. Before we pull our equipment, the pit should have a pump that works.

Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

This is one of the most misunderstood claims. A standard homeowners policy usually excludes water that backs up or overflows from a sump, and flood insurance is a separate policy with its own rules. Coverage often exists only if you added a water backup or sump overflow endorsement, and those carry their own limits. Your declarations page will list it, or your agent can tell you. Our records of the failed pump, how high the water rose and what it damaged go into your claim file.
