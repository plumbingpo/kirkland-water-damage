A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Pathogens in raw sewage include salmonella, giardia, hepatitis and E. coli, along with other viruses, parasites and bacteria, and contact with anything contaminated is a serious risk to health. When it happens in Kirkland, Kirkland Restoration is available 24/7 with technicians trained for this work, proper protective gear and disinfectants registered with the EPA, everything needed for a safe decontamination.

Sewage damage is classified as Category 3 (black water), the most contaminated and dangerous category of water damage. Because of it, cleanup follows the toughest rules in the trade, which means full protective gear, barriers to contain the area, tearing out any porous material that met sewage and disinfecting all that is left. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. Lingering sewage odor is eliminated with thermal fogging and industrial hydroxyl generators, which we run in the affected area.

Signs It Is Time for Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Soggy, smelly ground over the sewer line or leakage at the cleanout cap
  • Every sink, tub and shower draining slowly even after you plunge them
  • People in the household getting stomach or intestinal symptoms with no clear cause
  • Gurgling sounds from toilets or drains when other fixtures are used
  • Lower levels or drain openings giving off a heavy sewage odor, sometimes like rotten eggs
  • Wet spots on floors or walls accompanied by foul odors
  • Several fixtures backing up at once instead of only one

We start by measuring how far the sewage contamination reaches, noting any safety hazards and setting up containment so clean areas are not cross-contaminated.

Why Hire a Professional for Sewage Cleanup?

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. Hepatitis, gastrointestinal disease and infections of the skin or respiratory system are among the serious illnesses these pathogens cause.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. Putting carpet, insulation or drywall soaked with sewage out with household trash breaks the law and endangers sanitation workers and your community. Certified restoration companies maintain proper waste disposal protocols and relationships with licensed biohazard waste disposal facilities.

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Route sump pump and gutter water away from the sewer line, which can back up when storm flow overloads it
  • Toilet paper is the only thing to flush, and that rules out wipes sold as flushable, which still clog
  • Have roots removed where trees grow by the sewer, before they get inside the pipe and plug it
  • Every 2 to 3 years, get a sewer camera run through the line to spot developing issues
  • Find your sewer cleanout now and check its condition, so it can be reached in an emergency
  • A backwater valve at the sewer connection stops the city system from backing up into your home
